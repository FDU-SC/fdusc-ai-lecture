## (b) Per-topic detail

### 1. AlphaFold 3

**The access dispute is documented.** Reviewers got no code during peer review; the code-availability statement offered only a restricted server plus pseudocode. Signatories wrote that the 10-predictions/day cap "restricts the scientific community's capacity to verify the broad claims," and that pseudocode "will require months of effort to turn into workable code" ([Retraction Watch, 2024-05-14](https://retractionwatch.com/2024/05/14/nature-earns-ire-over-lack-of-code-availability-for-google-deepmind-protein-folding-paper/)). *Nature* had already editorialised: ["AlphaFold3 — why did Nature publish it without its code?"](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-024-01463-0).

**Licence friction has a documented downstream cost.** Fromm et al. could not extend AF3's sampling because "the AlphaFold3 license does not allow such modifications" — a primary-source admission that the licence constrained a published benchmark. Weights are governed by separate [WEIGHTS_TERMS_OF_USE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/alphafold3/main/WEIGHTS_TERMS_OF_USE.md) and [WEIGHTS_PROHIBITED_USE_POLICY.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/alphafold3/97639fff6fb22c0d9765089026fe296ee506b60a/WEIGHTS_PROHIBITED_USE_POLICY.md) files.

**Independent accuracy assessments — more favourable to AF3 than the brief assumes.**
- *CASP16 monomer assessment* ([bioRxiv 2025.05.29.656942](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.29.656942v1)): "no target folds were missed"; AF3 "demonstrated its superiority over AlphaFold2… particularly in confidence estimation and model selection." Progress from CASP15→16 was "very subtle," and **model ranking remains a persistent weakness**.
- *AlphaFold3 at CASP16* ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.10.648174v1.full); [Proteins 10.1002/prot.70044](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/prot.70044)): AF3 comparable to top methods; manual curation gained little; RNA and stoichiometry still weak. Ligand/affinity assessment: [Proteins 10.1002/prot.70061](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/prot.70061).
- *Fromm, Ludaic & Elofsson*: "AlphaFold3 outperforms AlphaFold2, Boltz-1, and Chai-1." AF3 **best-model** DockQ rises <0.3 → >0.5 at 200 samples, but **top-ranked** DockQ only 0.29 → 0.37 — "the internal ranking of the models cannot identify the best generated model." Boltz-1/Chai-1 top-ranked: 0.12 → 0.14. AF-Sample2 (AF2.3-ColMask) produced the best models overall, and "some targets [exist] where AlphaFold2.3 outperforms AlphaFold3." AF3 "declines significantly for complexes that lack structural similarity to the training set."
- *FoldBench* ([Nat. Commun. 10.1038/s41467-025-67127-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67127-3)): protein–ligand 558 targets, AF3 **64.9%** (~10 pts ahead); protein–protein 279 low-similarity complexes, AF3 **73.7%**; **antibody–antigen (n=172): most models fail >60%, AF3 best at 47.9%**; all models poor on allosteric sites and large RNA.
- Antibody-focused: [mAbs 2025, "What does AlphaFold3 learn about antibody and nanobody docking…"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/19420862.2025.2545601); AF3 failure on heterochiral D-peptide complexes: [bioRxiv 2025.03.14.643307](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.14.643307v3.full).

### 2. GNoME and A-Lab

A-Lab claimed 41/58 targets in 17 days (71%), raised to 43/58 (78%) with human intervention. Leeman, Liu, Stiles, Lee, Bhatt, Schoop & Palgrave ([PRX Energy 3, 011002](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXEnergy.3.011002); full text via [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10532478/media/xml)) audited all 43 and found "significant issues with 42 of them": "very poor and obviously incorrect fits" in **18/36** claimed successes; **two thirds** of successes are "likely to be known compositionally disordered versions of the predicted ordered compounds." Only three were correctly synthesized (MnAgO₂, Y₃In₂Ga₃O₁₂, CaFe₂(PO₄)₂O) — **all previously reported**. Bottom line: **3/58 ≈ 5%**, and under the usual novelty standard "none of the materials in the A-Lab paper would be counted as discoveries." They also record the Ceder group conceding A-Lab "does not live up to human standards," and note that "in the Google Deepmind GNoME paper, no mention at all is made of compositional disorder."

Cheetham & Seshadri ([Chem. Mater. 10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643)) reviewed the first 250 GNoME Explorer entries, found "scant evidence" for compounds meeting "novelty, credibility, and utility," objected that results are "solely… crystalline inorganic compounds" mislabelled "materials," and called the catalogue a "seemingly random walk through the periodic table." DeepMind "stands by all claims" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/11/google_deepmind_material_study/)). Companion editorial: [10.1021/acs.chemmater.3c03078](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/pdf/10.1021/acs.chemmater.3c03078).

**Resolution:** Nature issued an [Author Correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09992-y) on **19 January 2026** to the [original A-Lab paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w): the lab synthesized **36 of 57** targets, "new to the laboratory, not necessarily new to science." C&EN: correction leaves "some questions remain unanswered" ([C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/articles/104/web/2026/01/Nature-robot-chemist-paper-corrected.html)); earlier coverage: [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article).

### 3. Wet-lab validation fraction

**No systematic study quantifying this for AI-for-science as a whole was found.** The closest peer-reviewed number: Koutroumpa et al., *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2023 ([PMC10095548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10095548/), [10.3390/ijms24076573](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms24076573)) — PRISMA/PROSPERO review of deep-learning drug discovery with in vivo validation, Jan 2018–Apr 2022: 464 records → 283 deduplicated → 36 full-text → **12 included**; i.e. **12/36 = 33.3%** of screened and **12/283 = 4.2%** of deduplicated records. **Not a prevalence estimate** — in vivo validation was an *inclusion criterion*.

### 4. Concrete replication failures / exaggerated claims

1. **A-Lab** — 78% claim reduced by peer-reviewed audit to 5%, then narrowed by a 2026 Nature correction.
2. **GNoME "380,000 stable materials"** — critique found the claim rests on ordered structures where disorder is likelier; 0 K convex-hull "stability" ≠ synthesizable.
3. **AI drug success rates.** Jayatunga et al. (all five authors BCG affiliates), *Drug Discovery Today* 2024 ([PubMed 38692505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38692505/), [10.1016/j.drudis.2024.104009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drudis.2024.104009), open copy [Zenodo 13137004](https://zenodo.org/records/13137004)): Phase I **80–90%**, Phase II **~40%**; sample **67 AI-discovered molecules**, 24 claimed AI-discovered targets but only **3–9 novel**. Critique (opinion): Optibrium's Matt Segall puts the industry norm at **40–60%** and notes "only ten candidates… have reported results from Phase II trials. Four were successful" ([Optibrium](https://optibrium.com/knowledge-base/blog-is-ai-improving-drug-discovery-how/)). Derek Lowe's finding that **23 of 24** "AI-discovered" targets had literature precedent is **second-hand** ([Science blog, not retrievable](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/ai-drugs-so-far)).
4. **Retraction:** Retraction Watch, 2026-09-14 — *Technology, Mind, and Behavior* retracted a paper by an AI-company CEO on generative AI's cognitive impact ([Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/14/american-psychological-association-technology-mind-behavior-sarah-baldeo-id-quotient/)). An AI-*topic* retraction, **not** an AI-for-science method failure.

### 5. Meta-research

Kapoor & Narayanan ([Patterns 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804); [arXiv:2207.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)): published version reports **17 fields, collectively affecting 294 papers**; arXiv v1 reports **20 papers reviewed, 329 papers affected**, with **8** leakage types (no test set; preprocessing on train+test; feature selection on train+test; duplicates; illegitimate features; temporal leakage; non-independent train/test; sampling bias). **Materials science and climate science are not among the 17 fields.** Magnusson et al. (Findings of ACL 2023, [aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.809](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.809/)) analysed **10,405** checklist responses: only **46%** claimed open-source code; code availability associated with **+8%** reproducibility score. Raff's NeurIPS-2019 study: 255 papers ([arXiv:1909.06674](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06674)). **No ML Reproducibility Challenge report for 2023–2026 found.**

3. **The premise "AF3 is worse than AF2" is unsupported** for antibody–antigen work — Fromm et al. found the opposite. The only contrary evidence is [Commun. Chem. 10.1038/s42004-025-01763-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-025-01763-0) (large-scale allosteric transitions), which I read **only** via a Chinese-language summary reporting AF3's gain over AF2 as **not statistically significant** for autoinhibited proteins, with >10 Å errors on open states. Primary text and numbers **unverified**.
4. **GNoME paper DOI deliberately not cited** — I never retrieved it. All GNoME claims above are cited to the critiques.
5. **A-Lab correction specifics.** "36 of 57" and "new to the laboratory" come from a secondary tracker, not the correction itself; `nature.com` and `link.springer.com` both redirected to an identity provider.
6. **Halicin and Wong et al. 2023 ("structural class of antibiotics").** I found **no** documented replication failure, correction, or peer-reviewed critique for either. The common claim that halicin was previously reported as a c-Myc inhibitor is **unverified here**. Do not repeat a "failure" for these without a source.
7. **The ~33% AI-drug Phase II figure (3 of 9)** rests on one secondary aggregator ([ic.work](https://www.ic.work/article/ai-drug-discovery-phase-2-evidence-gap)); the primary source it cites resolves to an unrelated article. **Unverified.** The `s41573-026-01496-2` Nature Reviews Drug Discovery review is likewise unverified.
8. **No BCG-branded 2024/2025 report** with these numbers exists as far as I could find; the "BCG analysis" is the Jayatunga journal paper.
9. **No study** quantifying wet-lab validation across AI-for-science, AI-for-materials, or self-driving-lab literatures. A *Frontiers in Immunology* 2026 abstract exists ([link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1852829/abstract)) but its numbers are JS-rendered and unretrievable; a ChemRxiv preprint asserts "only a small fraction of studies report direct experimental validation" **with no percentage** ([ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/full/10.26434/chemrxiv.15001280/v2)).
10. **No AI-designed-protein replication failure and no correction/retraction of the Wong et al. 2023 antibiotics paper** was found. The "CRISPR/AI" and AI-protein-design categories yielded nothing verifiable and are **not** reported above.
