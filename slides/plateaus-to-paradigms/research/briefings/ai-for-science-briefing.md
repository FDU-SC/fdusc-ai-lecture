## 1. Mathematics

**AlphaProof, now peer-reviewed:** Hubert/Mehta/Sartran *et al.*, *Nature* **651:607–613**, 12 Nov 2025, DOI [10.1038/s41586-025-09833-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09833-y) **[PR]**. RL inside Lean after auto-formalizing **80M statements**; AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry solved **4/6** IMO 2024 = silver (28/42). Authors flag limits on other problem classes. [Press release](http://www.natureasia.com/en/info/press-releases/detail/9147). **AlphaGeometry2** ([2502.03544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03544) **[PP]**): **84%** on IMO geometry 2000–2024 vs 54% — on *historical* problems.

**IMO 2025 dispute.** OpenAI announced **18 Jul 2025**: unreleased model, **35/42**. DeepMind **21 Jul**: Gemini Deep Think also **35/42**, but **graded by IMO judges**, certified by IMO President **Gregor Dolinar**, inside the 4.5-hour limit, natural language end-to-end. DeepMind's Thang Luong: the IMO uses a **non-public scoring guide**, without which a medal claim is unverifiable; Hassabis said labs were asked to wait. OpenAI's Noam Brown: OpenAI *declined* official collaboration, no tools, graded by **three IMO gold medallists** after the ceremony. **72 humans gold, 5 perfect 42** ([eu.36kr.com](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3389556081328263) **[JOUR]**; imo-official.org unreachable, so no primary IMO statement obtained).

**IMO 2026 (Shanghai, 15–16 Jul)** ran a formal AI track: problems released to models only *after* each day's human session; **all human intervention prohibited**. Xiaohongshu's **dots-note 3.0** took original LaTeX problems via an agentic natural-language + Python system and got **7/7 × 6 = 42/42** from IMO graders — the **first perfect AI score in IMO history**, 13 points above the 29-point gold cut; 7 of 600+ humans scored 42 ([China Daily](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202607/22/WS6a607452a310d709c2fbf0ec.html); [WIC](http://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/wic/2026-07/25/c_1200427.htm) **[JOUR]**). **Do not conflate:** Anthropic's **Claude Opus 5 at 42/42** is from its own System Card §8.6 — four no-tool solutions per problem, all 24 accepted by a **three-model panel**, humans awarding one pre-specified solution per problem full credit ([benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/imo2026) **[JOUR]**) — model-panel self-grading, **not** IMO grading.

**2026 open-problem results (all preprint).** **AlphaProof Nexus** ([2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763) **[PP]**, 21 May 2026): autonomously resolved **9 of 353 open Erdős problems** at **a few hundred USD each**, proved **44 of 492 OEIS conjectures**, deployed in combinatorics, optimization, graph theory, algebraic geometry and quantum optics; two Erdős problems date from 1970 (**open 56 years**). Lean proofs at `github.com/google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results`. Caveats: a *basic* agent alternating LLM generation with Lean verification reproduced the successes — compiler feedback did the work; 9/353 ≈ 2.5%, matching **Terence Tao's** ~1–2% public estimate ([qbitai](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/05/425455.html) **[JOUR]**). Aarhus University confirms co-author **Gergely Bérczi** validated the solutions ([math.au.dk](https://math.au.dk/en/currently/news/news-item/artikel/gergely-deepmind) **[OFF]**). **OpenAI** reported a model producing, **without human guidance**, a proof **refuting** Erdős's 1946 unit-distance conjecture, verified by **nine mathematicians including Tim Gowers** ([lesnumeriques](https://www.lesnumeriques.com/intelligence-artificielle/openai-une-ia-a-resolu-un-probleme-de-maths-irresolu-depuis-1946-en-toute-autonomie-n256294.html) **[JOUR]**) — **no paper found**. Cleanest machine-checked case: [2604.03782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03782) **[PP]** resolves whether the exact O(1/t) last-iterate rate is achievable for anchored gradient descent-ascent — "**discovered autonomously by an AI system capable of writing formal proofs in Lean**".

**FrontierMath:** SOTA **under 2%** at launch ([2411.04872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872) **[PP]**); DeepMind's **AI co-mathematician** reports **48% on Tier 4** ([2605.06651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06651) **[PP]**) — self-reported. "GPT-6 Astra sweeps Tier 4" is **unverified**.

**Lean/mathlib (2026-09-16):** **136,850 definitions, 288,518 theorems, 772 contributors** ([mathlib_stats](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib_stats.html) **[OFF]**), ~2M lines. Anthropic **formalised Fermat's Last Theorem** in 11 days — **30,300 theorems, 13M lines Lean**, reviewed by Kevin Buzzard; Math Inc's **Gauss** formalised Viazovska sphere packing in dims 8 and 24, maintainer-verified. Both formalise *known* theorems. **Supply-chain warning:** Trail of Bits disclosed a Lean `native_decide` unsoundness (9 Sep 2026) allowing a forged "proof"; kernel soundness intact. **Tao** co-authored [2607.07779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07779) **[PP]**, arguing systems "remain fundamentally limited in tackling frontier research mathematics… open-ended, under-specified".

## 2. Biology, medicine, chemistry

**AlphaFold 3** (8 May 2024) shipped **no code, weights or data**: a server capped at **10 predictions/day**, restricted ligands, plus pseudocode. A letter to *Nature* (14 May 2024, organised by Stephanie Wankowicz; [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/11391920) **[OFF]**) said the paper "advertises capabilities that remain locked behind the doors of the parent company"; reviewer **Roland Dunbrack** was denied code "despite repeated requests" ([Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2024/05/14/nature-earns-ire-over-lack-of-code-availability-for-google-deepmind-protein-folding-paper/) **[JOUR]**). Code/weights arrived **Nov 2024** under a **non-commercial licence** — the licence, not the delay, is the live complaint; Fromm *et al.* could not extend AF3's sampling because "the AlphaFold3 license does not allow such modifications".

**Drop "AF3 is worse than AF2".** On **110 unseen antibody–antigen complexes**, AF3 **outperforms** AF2, Boltz-1 and Chai-1 ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.11.662141v2.full) **[PP]**). The real failure is **ranking**: *best-model* DockQ rises <0.3→>0.5 across 200 samples but *top-ranked* DockQ reaches only 0.37. **FoldBench** (1,522 complexes, [Nat. Commun.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67127-3) **[PR]**): AF3 leads protein–ligand (64.9%) and low-similarity protein–protein (73.7%), but **antibody–antigen fails >60% for most models, AF3 best at 47.9%**.

**Best 2026 co-folding result is negative.** Wan/Zhang/Xue/Coveney ([2603.05532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05532) **[PP]**) evaluated **Boltz-2** on **16,780 compounds (3CLPro) and 21,702 (TNKS2)**: multiple conformations rather than one converged pose; affinity correlations "only weak to moderate"; among top-100 compounds **no significant correlation** with physics-based ESMACS free energies — "lacks the energetic resolution required for lead identification". Their framing: "no 'AI-discovered drugs' have so far received regulatory approval." **Latent-X** ([2507.19375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19375) **[PP]**): **>90% experimental hit rates on macrocyclic peptides** from **30–100 designs per target** (developer-run wet lab).

**ESM3** appeared in *Science* **21 Feb 2025**, artefact **esmGFP** ([EvolutionaryScale](https://www.evolutionaryscale.ai/blog/esm3-release) **[OFF]**). I could **not** verify "% identity to nearest known FP", and found **no evidence of an "ESM4" release**.

**AI antibiotics — strongest wet-lab result here.** Krishnan/Anahtar/Valeri *et al.*, **Cell 188(21):5962–5979.e22**, online **14 Aug 2025**, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2025.07.033 **[PR]** ([record](https://publications.scilifelab.se/publication/cb92a5fa72ae4cd6953839297ce2af22)). **Of 24 synthesised compounds, 7 selectively antibacterial; 2 leads bactericidal against multidrug-resistant isolates** and **reduced bacterial burden in mouse models**. No independent replication, but **no documented failure** either.

**AI drugs in the clinic.** **Rentosertib (ISM001-055)**, Insilico: AI-designed molecule against **TNIK**, an AI-found target previously unlinked to fibrosis. 2024 *Nat. Biotech.* → 2025 *Nature Medicine* (Phase IIa) → 2026 *Nat. Biotech.*: six independent proteomic aging clocks, 42 IPF patients, peak **Week 4, 30 mg BID, ~3–4 years biological-age reversal (up to 6 on one clock)**, dose-dependent FVC improvement ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1142848) **[OFF]**). **9 Sep 2026**: first patient dosed in **GENESIS-IPF-3 (NCT07687459)** — the **first Phase III trial of a generative-AI-discovered drug** ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1143340) **[OFF]**, "Business Announcement"). **Isomorphic Labs** had **not** begun trials; reporting indicates slippage to **late 2026**. **AAV gene therapy design: not verified.**

## 3. Physics, weather, climate

**Skill numbers.** **GraphCast** (Science 2023, DOI 10.1126/science.adi2336; [2212.12794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794) **[PP]**): 10-day 0.25° forecast in **<1 min**, beating the best operational deterministic systems on **90% of 1,380 targets** — against *retrospective ERA5*. **GenCast** (Nature 637:84–90): beats ECMWF **ENS** on **97.2% of 1,320 targets**, 15 days/12-hourly/0.25° in **8 min** (preprint: **97.4%**). **NeuralGCM** (`s41586-024-07744-y`) matches ECMWF ENS at 1–15 days and is stable multi-decade; **Aurora** (`s41586-025-09005-y`) covers air quality, waves and tropical cyclones; **Aardvark** (`s41586-025-08897-0`) is end-to-end from observations, skilful to 10 days. **Caveats:** against *station observations* AI weather errors run **15–45% larger** than against reanalysis ([2509.01879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01879) **[PP]**); coverage degrades on extremes ([2606.19642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19642) **[PP]**). No agency publishes exact AIFS-vs-IFS RMSE/CRPS/ACC.

**Shipped operationally, but has not replaced physics.** ECMWF: **AIFS Single** operational **25 Feb 2025**, **AIFS ENS** (51 members) **1 Jul 2025** — **31 km vs 9 km**, gains up to **20%** on surface temperature, **>10× faster**, **~1000× less energy**, still using physics-based data assimilation ([ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ensemble-ai-forecasts-become-operational) **[OFF]**). Both to **v2 on 12 May 2026**, when ECMWF **stopped running** Aurora, FourCastNet, GraphCast and Pangu. **DWD** has run **AICON** operationally since **2 Mar 2026** ([2608.24651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24651) **[PP]**). AIFS still runs **alongside** IFS: no agency has retired physics NWP. On **operational** AIFS ENS vs IFS ENS for **10 m wind** over >9,000 stations (Jul–Nov 2025), **raw IFS was substantially superior at all lead times** ([2606.02508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02508) **[PP]**). **RealBench** ([2605.24945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24945) **[PP]**) argues ERA5-based benchmarking is systematically misleading; **ObsCast** ([2605.28153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28153) **[PP]**) uses **no NWP-derived data** yet beats operational NWP for near-surface variables through 18 h. Google's **WeatherNext 2** (17 Nov 2025) is **vendor-claim only**.

**Fusion.** Verified: Degrave *et al.* (Nature 2022) — RL commands TCV's full coil set across elongated, negative-triangularity and snowflake configurations. **Common mis-citation corrected:** Seo *et al.* (Nature 626:746–751, 21 Feb 2024) avoided tearing instability while maintaining H-mode — on **DIII-D, not KSTAR**. For **2025–2026** fusion RL I have **nothing solid**; treat that window as unresolved. **Lattice QCD:** DOIs exist (PRD 110, 034517; PoS Lattice 2025 027) but **no quantitative speedups** retrievable.

**CERN — the most concrete AI-in-production numbers here.** The **CMS Level-1/Global Trigger** takes **40 MHz** within a **50 ns** budget; an autoencoder is deployed in the GT test crate, validated on live Run 3 data ([2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19506) **[PP]**). The 2026 flow-distillation result ([2609.15295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15295) **[PP]**) reaches **27–52 ns** FPGA latency at **~325× compression**, 8-bit, DSP/BRAM-free. **hls4ml** radiation-hard backend: **25 ns** on Microchip PolarFire (LHCb PicoCal; [2602.15751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15751) **[PR]**); ATLAS GEP ML blocks at **1.2 µs** in an **8 ms** budget ([2406.12875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12875) **[PR]**).

**Physics benchmarks are unreliable in both directions.** **CritPt**: best base model GPT-5(high) **5.7%**, ~10% with tools ([2509.26574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26574) **[PP]**). **PHYBench**: Gemini 2.5 Pro **36.9%** vs experts **61.9%** ([2504.16074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16074) **[PP]**). But the Sept 2026 audit ([2609.13009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13009) **[PP]**) found reported scores **understated** — most apparent model errors were grader or reference-solution faults: GPT-5.6-Sol mean@4 **47.3%→78.7%** (HLE-Physics), **61.0%→87.2%** (CMT-Bench).

## 4. Materials and hard-science infrastructure

**Release dates and data provenance** (all **[PP]** unless noted): M3GNet [2202.02450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02450), Feb 2022, peer-reviewed *Nat. Comput. Sci.* 2022, trained on MP relaxation trajectories; CHGNet [2302.14231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14231), Feb 2023, pretrained on **MPtrj ≈1.5M inorganic structures incl. magnetic moments** (peer-reviewed venue not confirmed); **MACE-MP-0** [2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00096), Dec 2023, trained on **MPtrj (146k→1.58M frames)**; **MACE-MPA-0** [2405.07105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07105), May 2024, = MP-0b3 architecture retrained on **MPtrj + sAlex (3.37M→12M)**; **MatterSim** [2405.04967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04967), May 2024, 0–5000 K and ≤1000 GPa, own dataset; **Orb-v2** [2410.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22570), Oct 2024; **Orb-v3** [2504.06231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06231), Apr 2025, ">10× reduction in latency and >8× reduction in memory", trained on **MPtrj + Alexandria + OMat24 (6.47M→133M)**; **eSEN** [2502.12147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12147), Feb 2025, selected by an energy-conservation-in-MD test rather than held-out error; **GRACE** [2508.17936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17936), Aug 2025; **Allegro-FM** [2502.06073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06073), Feb 2025. **UMA** (Meta FAIR, [2506.23971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23971) **[PP]**, Jun 2025 / v2 Mar 2026) trains on **half a billion unique 3D atomic structures** — "the largest training runs to date" — compiling molecular, materials and catalyst data; UMA-medium has **1.4B parameters but only ~50M active per structure**, and one un-finetuned model matches or beats specialists.

**Compute cost — now quantified.** **MACE-MP-0** ([2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00096v3) **[PP]**): MP-0a trained 200 epochs on **40–80 A100s across 10–20 nodes**; MP-0b3 99 epochs on **32 H100s × 8 nodes**; "training the medium-sized model took approx. **2,600 GPU hours**". **UMA** ([2506.23971](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23971v1) **[PP]**, Table 8): UMA-M = **129,024 H200 GPU-hours** (128×14 d + 256×14 d); UMA-S 46,080; UMA-L 95,232 H100-hours. **Data generation dwarfs training — and the dataset sizes are now pinned down** (all verified 2026-09-16): **Materials Project 154,387 structures** (35,388 with E_hull ≤ 0; PBE/PBE+U; DB v2026.04.13, [OPTIMADE endpoint](https://optimade.materialsproject.org/v1/structures?page_limit=1)) — **do not use the commonly quoted "~170k/200k"**; **OMat24 111M structures** (train split 100,824,585; PBE and PBE+U; CC 4.0) and **OMol25 >140M DFT single-point calculations, ~83M unique systems, 83 elements** (ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD; CC BY 4.0) — these two cost **400M+ core-hours** and **6.6 billion CPU core-hours** respectively ([2410.12771v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12771v2), [2505.08762v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08762v2) **[PP]**); **Alexandria 5.8M structures**, 175k on the convex hull (PBE/PBEsol/SCAN, [2512.09169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09169) **[PP]**); **sAlex 10M train + 0.5M val, sAlex25 14M** (same paper — note "sAlex ≈ 6M" is wrong); **MPtrj ~1.5M**; MatPES ~400,000 (PBE + r2SCAN, [2503.04070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04070) **[PP]**). **OQMD's own total is unverifiable** (oqmd.org does not resolve); the 568,000 figure is a *LeMat-Bulk post-exclusion* count, not OQMD's total. Dataset *quality* is itself now a literature: LeMat-Bulk documents duplicates and provenance problems across MP/OQMD/Alexandria ([2511.05178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05178) **[PP]**) and MLIP Arena documents benchmark data leakage ([2509.20630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20630) **[PP]**). **eSEN-30M-MP** = **335 A100 GPU-days** (reported in [2606.02419](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02419v3) **[PP]**). **GNoME: no total GPU-hour figure exists** — the Nature paper reports only **four TPU v3 chips**, so the circulating "~5 million GPU-hours" claim is **unverifiable**. **Inference** (steps/s on one H100 at 100 / 1k / 10k atoms, UMA Table 3): ORB-v3-conservative-inf-omat 77/30/3.7, UMA-S 44/16/1.6, MACE-MPA-0 38/24/2.9, eSEN-30M-OAM 8/1.7/OOM.

**On "millions of times faster than DFT": no primary source makes that claim.** M3GNet reports a relaxation in **22 s on one laptop CPU core vs 15 h on 32 cores** for the equivalent DFT (~7.9×10⁴ in core-seconds); "at least four orders of magnitude faster" for SiO₂ phonons is acknowledged by the authors as **apples-to-oranges** (frozen phonon vs DFPT, different supercells) ([2202.02450](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.02450v2) **[PP]**). A 23-model benchmark makes the methodological point explicitly ([2607.07647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07647) **[PP]**): DFT cost grows ~cubically with atom count while fixed-cutoff MLFFs scale near-linearly, so "DFT-normalized speedups provide a practical reference scale, but the more rigorous comparison is between MLFFs and classical force fields under the same atomistic workload" — on a 192-atom LiCoO₂/PBE baseline, Buckingham ~**11,600×**, 2NNMEAM+QEq ~**533×**, lightweight MLIPs "hundreds to thousands", and **the heaviest MLIPs under 2×**.

**The independent benchmark is Matbench Discovery** — now peer-reviewed (*Nat. Mach. Intell.* **7**(6):836–847, 2025-06-23, DOI [10.1038/s42256-025-01055-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01055-1) **[PR]**; Riebesell *et al.*, [2308.14920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14920) **[PP]**). **CPS is the "Combined Performance Score" = discovery F1 (50%) + geometry RMSD (10%) + phonon κ_SRME (40%)** on unique-prototype discovery scores, excluding MD and diatomics — *not* a throughput metric. ## 5. Formal verification, theorem proving, chip design

**The AI-prover literature has an honesty gap.** Reported numbers — DeepSeek-Prover-V2 **88.9%** miniF2F, Goedel-Prover-V2 **84.6%** pass@32, Seed-Prover **78.1%** formalised-IMO with 5/6 at IMO 2025 — collapse under end-to-end evaluation: **miniF2F-Lean Revisited** ([2511.03108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108) **[PP]**) finds **~36%** vs 97%/69% when autoformalization and proving are scored separately. **Autoformalization, not proving, is the bottleneck.**

**Industry adoption is real but thin.** Anchors: **Arm Morello-Cerise** (Hammond *et al.*, **PLDI 2025**, DOI [10.1145/3729329](https://doi.org/10.1145/3729329)) — machine-checked strong-encapsulation proof at full-scale ISA specification; **AWS Cedar-in-Lean** (8 Apr 2024) and Amazon's investment in the Lean FRO; EPFL "Agentic Proof Automation" ([2601.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03768) **[PP]**: **189 tasks**, **87%** success, **16%** human intervention — academic). No audited 2025–26 metrics from Intel or AMD. Synopsys claims **Synopsys.ai Copilot** (4 Sep 2025) gives a **35% formal-verification productivity gain** via an **unnamed** customer — vendor marketing.

**LLM RTL is weaker than leaderboards suggest.** VerilogEval v2 ([2408.11053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11053) **[PP]**): GPT-4o **63%** spec→RTL. **GateTruth** ([2608.12635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12635) **[PP]**, 12 Aug 2026) mutation-tests **RTLLM v2.0**: of 46 auditable designs, **72% fall below a 95% mutant-kill floor, three score 0%**; NVIDIA's **CVDP** is unauditable (reference solutions withheld). **No verified production deployment of LLM-generated RTL.**

**AlphaChip: genuine, unresolved dispute.** Crossref change history for `10.1038/s41586-021-03544-w`: Nature 2021-06-09 (594:207–212) → Correction 2022-03-31 → **Editor's Note 2023-09-20** ("performance claims… called into question") → 2024-09-26 Note **removed**, **Addendum** published, renamed **AlphaChip**. Critiques: Cheng/Kahng *et al.* ([2302.11014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11014) **[PP]**, IEEE TCAD) — a stronger simulated-annealing baseline gets up to **26% better proxy cost at equal runtime with a quarter of the resources**; a commercial placer beats circuit-training-with-pre-training by **34% routed wirelength**. Markov, "The False Dawn" ([2306.09633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09633) **[PP]**): Google RL lags humans, SA and commercial tools while being slower; **not top-5** in the 2023 MLCAD contest; "the integrity of the Nature paper is substantially undermined". Rebuttal: Goldie/Mirhoseini/Dean ([2411.10053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10053) **[PP]**): the critique did not run the method as described (**20× fewer** RL experience collectors, no pre-training). **Production evidence is exclusively Google's own.** "Samsung uses AlphaChip" is a **misreading** — MediaTek's Dimensity, designed with AlphaChip, is used *in Samsung phones*.

## 6. Critiques and limits

**The strongest documented failure is the A-Lab, not AlphaFold 3.** A-Lab claimed **41/58**, raised to **43/58 (78%)**. Leeman, Liu, Stiles, Lee, Bhatt, Schoop & Palgrave audited all 43 in **PRX Energy 3, 011002** (2024) **[PR]**: "significant issues with 42 of them"; "very poor and obviously incorrect fits" in **18/36**; **two-thirds** of successes "likely to be known compositionally disordered versions of the predicted ordered compounds". Only three (MnAgO₂, Y₃In₂Ga₃O₁₂, CaFe₂(PO₄)₂O) were correctly synthesised — **all previously reported**. Real rate **3/58 = 5%**; under the usual novelty standard "none of the materials in the A-Lab paper would be counted as discoveries". Nature's **Author Correction (19 Jan 2026)** narrowed it to **36 of 57**, "new to the laboratory, not necessarily new to science".

**GNoME's 380,000 "stable" materials.** Cheetham & Seshadri (`10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643` **[PR]**) found "scant evidence" for novelty, credibility and utility in the first 250 GNoME Explorer entries; called the catalogue a "seemingly random walk through the periodic table". Deeper issue: GNoME **never mentions compositional disorder**, and 0 K convex-hull stability is not synthesizability.

**Wet-lab validation fraction: nobody has measured it.** I found **no systematic study** across AI-for-science, AI-for-materials or self-driving-lab literature. Closest peer-reviewed figure: Koutroumpa *et al.*, *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2023 ([PMC10095548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10095548/)) — 464 records → 283 deduplicated → 36 full-text → **12 included**, i.e. **12/36 = 33.3%** and **12/283 = 4.2%** — but in vivo validation was an *inclusion criterion*, so this is **not a prevalence estimate**. Say "no reliable estimate exists".

**AI drug success rates are disputed.** Jayatunga *et al.* (all five authors BCG affiliates), *Drug Discovery Today* 2024: Phase I **80–90%**, Phase II **~40%**, on **67 molecules** and 24 claimed AI-discovered targets of which only **3–9 were genuinely novel**. Industry commentary puts the norm at **40–60%**. Sample tiny and selected; the circulating "~33% Phase II" variant traces to one aggregator whose cited source does not support it.

**Meta-research.** Kapoor & Narayanan (*Patterns* 2023): data leakage across **17 fields affecting 294 papers** — **materials and climate science are not among the 17**. Magnusson *et al.* (Findings of ACL 2023): **10,405** checklist responses, only **46%** claimed open-source code; code availability associated with **+8%** reproducibility.

**Explicitly unverified — do not assert:** (1) AF3 code-release date (11 Nov 2024) and exact licence clauses; (2) any AF3 open-letter signatory count ("650+" appeared only in a Chinese aggregator); (3) any documented replication failure for **halicin** or Wong *et al.* 2023 — searched specifically, found **none**; (4) Isomorphic Labs trial timing; (5) any AI-designed AAV capsid result; (6) 2025–26 experimental tokamak RL results; (7) AlphaChip production use beyond Google's own statements; (8) OpenAI's Erdős unit-distance proof (press-report only); (9) "GPT-6 Astra sweeps FrontierMath Tier 4"; (10) exact AIFS-vs-IFS RMSE/CRPS/ACC; (11) quantitative lattice-QCD speedups; (12) GNoME training compute (~"5M GPU-hours"), **OQMD's own current total** (568k is a LeMat-Bulk post-exclusion figure; oqmd.org does not resolve), OMat24's unique-composition count, peer-reviewed journal venues for OMat24/OMol25 (arXiv tech reports only), CHGNet's peer-reviewed venue, and any independent replication of the current #1 CPS entry Prophet-OAME-MBD (vendor PDF only, no arXiv); (13) the *Nature Machine Intelligence* editorial on AI and mathematics (`s42256-026-01269-x`) — located but unreadable here.

**Three claims worth the lecture's weight:** (a) AI now produces *machine-checked, genuinely new* mathematics — AlphaProof Nexus's 9 Erdős problems and the Lean-verified anchored-GDA rate; (b) AI-driven science has shipped in operational infrastructure — ECMWF AIFS ENS, DWD AICON, CMS L1 triggers at 27–52 ns — while **not** yet beating physics-based NWP where measured operationally; (c) the field's most instructive episode is the **A-Lab correction**: 78% audited down to 5%, then formally narrowed by Nature in January 2026.
