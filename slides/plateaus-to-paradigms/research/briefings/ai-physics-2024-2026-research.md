## (b) Per-topic detail

**1. Weather.** Operational reality: AIFS runs *alongside* IFS, not instead of it — a joint IFS 50r1 + AIFS v2 upgrade on 2026-05-12 ([ECMWF wiki](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/FCST/Implementation+of+AIFS+ENS+v2)). ECMWF's own v2 page concedes "small 'skill' degradations at longer lead times for some variables." AIFS is 6-hourly to 15 days at N320 (~31 km), 00/06/12/18 UTC. The AIFS paper is still a **preprint** ([arXiv:2406.01465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01465)). 2026 successors are preprints at 1.5°: U-Cast, Otter Weather (+9.6% vs best NWP baseline at 24 h; Otter-XL +9.7% CRPS vs IFS ENS, >2% better than GenCast, [arXiv:2606.26421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26421)), an NVIDIA framework ([arXiv:2601.18111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18111)), GenCast distillation at 7.5× speedup ([arXiv:2606.30414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30414)), TCBench ([arXiv:2601.23268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23268)). Independent checks temper the headline: against *station observations* rather than reanalysis, AI errors were 15–45% larger ([arXiv:2509.01879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01879)); GenCast/NeuralGCM/AIFS-ENS statistical coverage degrades on extremes ([arXiv:2606.19642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19642)). **No agency has retired physics-based NWP.**

**2. Fusion.** Degrave et al. (Nature 602, 414–419, Feb 2022) trained an RL controller commanding TCV's full coil set, producing elongated, negative-triangularity and snowflake configurations with accurate location/current/shape tracking, plus sustained "droplets" (two plasmas at once) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04301-9). Seo et al. (Nature 626, 746–751, 2024-02-21) avoided tearing instability with RL trained inside a learned multimodal dynamics model, holding tearing likelihood under threshold at low safety factor and low torque while maintaining H-mode — but the experiment was on **DIII-D**, not KSTAR ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07024-9)). This corrects a widespread misattribution.

**3. Particle physics.** CMS's Global Trigger decides at **40 MHz within a 50 ns** latency budget; an autoencoder was integrated into the GT test crate and validated on live Run 3 collisions ([arXiv:2411.19506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19506), PoS ICHEP2024 1025). A 2026 normalising-flow distillation achieves ~**325×** compression, 8-bit weights (AUC shift <0.06 pp), student within 0.1 pp AUC of teacher, FPGA latency **27–52 ns**, DSP/BRAM-free ([arXiv:2609.15295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15295)). hls4ml got a radiation-hard backend: **25 ns** latency on Microchip PolarFire (LHCb PicoCal), peer-reviewed [Mach. Learn. Sci. Tech. 7, 045024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15751); transformer inference numbers in [JINST 20 P04014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05207). ATLAS's Global Event Processor has an **8 ms** budget, with hls4ml/fwX ML blocks at **1.2 µs** and <5% resources ([JINST 19 P05031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12875)). WOMBAT: DNN L1 jet-substructure trigger ([arXiv:2505.05532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05532)). CMS L1 scouting now has HL-LHC baselines and physics results (JINST 21 C01046, C01024).

**4. Quantum many-body / lattice QCD.** The doped-2D-Hubbard NQS result is now peer-reviewed in *Nature Communications* (2026-06-22) and reports half-filled stripe order matching cuprate experiment ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74028-6)). Lattice QCD ML is preconditioner- and flow-based: gauge-equivariant pooling layers ([PRD 110, 034517](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevd.110.034517), 2024-08-28) and a new architecture (PoS Lattice 2025, 2026-04-06), descended from equivariant flow sampling ([PRL 125, 121601](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.125.121601)). MLIP foundation model: MACE-MP-0 ([arXiv:2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00096)). **Disputed claims:** GNoME's "2.2M crystals / 380k stable" *Nature* paper ([s41586-023-06735-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06735-9)) drew a formal published critique ([Chem. Mater., 2024-04-08](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643)); the A-Lab "41 new materials" claim ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w)) was widely challenged.

**5. Benchmarks.** CritPt: **71** composite research challenges from 50+ active physicists, 190 checkpoints; best base model (GPT-5 high) only **5.7%**, ~10% with tools ([arXiv:2509.26574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26574)). PHYBench: 500 original problems; Gemini 2.5 Pro **36.9%** vs experts **61.9%** ([arXiv:2504.16074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16074)). HLE: **2,500** questions ([arXiv:2501.14249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249)). GPQA: 448 questions; PhD experts 65%, non-experts 34%, GPT-4 39% ([arXiv:2311.12022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022)). The 2026 audit above shows reported physics scores are substantially **understated** because of broken grading.
