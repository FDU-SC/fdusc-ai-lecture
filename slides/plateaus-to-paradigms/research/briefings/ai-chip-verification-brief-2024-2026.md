## (b) Per-topic detail

**1. AlphaChip and the dispute.** Google's own change log is the evidence trail: code unavailable (2021-10-26), Correction (2022-03-31), Editor's Note (2023-09-20), then removal plus Addendum (2024-09-26) ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41586-021-03544-w)). Cheng/Kahng et al. built a stronger simulated-annealing baseline ("go-with-the-winners" + multithreading) achieving **up to 26% better proxy cost at equal runtime using a quarter of the resources**; a commercial placer beat pretrained Circuit Training by **34% routed wirelength on BlackParrot-GF12**; SA won proxy cost in **6 of 9** cases (CT-AC 2, CT-scratch 1); CT's **scalability and pre-training methodology** remain unconfirmed ([arXiv:2302.11014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11014)). Markov concludes RL lags humans, simulated annealing and commercial tools while being slower, and calls the Addendum a repetition whose "results on one additional proprietary TPU block with undisclosed statistics do not support any serious conclusions" ([arXiv:2306.09633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09633)). **Production evidence is exclusively Google's own**: trade press reads per-generation figures off Google's charts (v5e ≈10 blocks/3.2% wirelength reduction; Trillium ≈25 blocks/6.2%), which I could not confirm against the blocked addendum text. No independent verification from TSMC, MediaTek or third parties.

**2. LLM RTL generation.** Benchmarks are real, ceilings modest (63% GPT-4o spec→RTL, 2024), and the 2026 *GateTruth* audit shows the benchmarks themselves are weakly instrumented: most RTLLM v2.0 testbenches catch <95% of injected mutants, three catch none ([arXiv:2608.12635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12635)). ChipNeMo is an internal assistant, not a taped-out generator ([arXiv:2311.00176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00176)); Chip-Chat explored GPT-4 co-design with a SkyWater 130nm tapeout ([arXiv:2305.13243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13243)). **No verified production deployment** of LLM-generated RTL found.

**3. Theorem proving.** Peer-reviewed anchor: AlphaProof, **Nature** 651:607–613 (2025-11-12). 2025 preprints cluster at 78–89% on miniF2F, but end-to-end performance falls to ~36% once comprehension and autoformalization are counted ([arXiv:2511.03108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03108)). 2026 moved to open problems (AlphaProof Nexus) and large formalizations (FLT, sphere packing); the latter two **formalize known theorems**, while AlphaProof Nexus is the exception, reporting resolved open problems. Calibration: PFR's *mathematics* is peer-reviewed in Annals ([doi](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2025.201.2.5)) while its Lean artifact is not, and the "sphere eversion project (2025)" does not exist — its paper is CPP 2023 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3573105.3575688)). Mathlib is ~2M lines, 750+ contributors ([Formal Frontier](https://mathlib-initiative.org/formal-frontier/)).

**4. Industrial formal verification.** Thin but not empty: Arm's Morello-Cerise proof at full-scale ISA spec (peer-reviewed, PLDI 2025), AWS's Cedar work in Lean, and the EPFL agentic-proof case study — the only measured AI-in-a-real-Lean-codebase result I found (87% of 189 tasks; 16% needed humans). FVEval benchmarks LLMs on formal-verification tasks but measures no deployment. Synopsys claims 30% faster ramp, 10×–20× faster script generation and a 35% formal-verification productivity gain via an **unnamed** customer ([trade press, 2025-09-04](https://embeddedcomputing.com/technology/ai-machine-learning/ai-logic-devices-worload-acceleration/synopsys-expands-synopsysai-copilot-with-new-genai-capabilities-to-accelerate-semiconductor-design)) — vendor-sourced, unaudited. No independently audited, quantified 2025–2026 deployment metrics for AI-assisted formal verification exist in what I could reach.
