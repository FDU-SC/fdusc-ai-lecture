# Frontier AI Models & Evaluation Benchmarks, Early 2025 – September 2026

**Research briefing for a technical lecture.** Compiled 2026-09-16. Confidence labels: **[P]** = primary source fetched (arXiv paper, model card, official leaderboard, lab blog); **[S]** = secondary outlet reporting a number or quoting a named person; **[!]** = could not verify — do not put on a slide without re-checking.

**Method caveat.** `web_search`/`web_fetch` were intermittently down during collection, and `openai.com`, `anthropic.com`, `huggingface.co`, `lmarena.ai` and `blog.google` were bot-blocked or unreachable from this environment. Claims originating from those labs are therefore often labelled **[S]** even though the underlying source is the lab itself. **LMArena ranks could not be verified at all** — the single biggest hole in this briefing.

## 1. Frontier model releases, 2025–2026

### OpenAI
GPT-4.5 (2025-02-27) → **o3/o4-mini** (2025-04-16) → **GPT-5** (2025-08-07) → GPT-5.1 (2025-11-12) → **GPT-5.2** (2025-12-11, the "code red" release after Gemini 3) → GPT-5.3-Codex → **GPT-5.4** (2026-03-05) → **GPT-5.5** (2026-04-23) → **GPT-5.6 Sol / Terra / Luna** (2026-07-08/09) → **GPT-6 Astra** (2026-09-02/04). The release cadence compressed to roughly six weeks between 5.4 and 5.5. GPT-5.5: 1M context, $5/$30 per M tokens (double GPT-5.4), 82.7% Terminal-Bench 2.0, 58.6% SWE-bench Pro, High-risk classification for bio and cyber. GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's first model to cross the "critical" cybersecurity threshold and is marketed with explicit AGI framing. [S] [NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/openai-releases-gpt-5-5-agentic-coding-ceiling-tops-14-benchmarks/) / [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/05/openai-new-model-gpt5-4-enterprise-agentic-anthropic/) / [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4218691/openai-launches-gpt-6-astra-its-first-model-to-cross-a-critical-cybersecurity-threshold-3.html). **No OpenAI model in this window has a disclosed parameter count or training FLOP budget.** **[P] verified absence.**

### Anthropic
Claude 3.7 Sonnet (2025-02-24) → Claude 4 Opus/Sonnet (2025-05-22) → Opus 4.1 (2025-08-05) → Sonnet 4.5 (2025-09-29) → Haiku 4.5 (2025-10-15) → **Opus 4.5** (2025-11-24) → **Opus 4.6** (~2026-02-05) → **Opus 4.7** (~2026-04) → **Opus 4.8** → **Mythos Preview** (2026-04-07, restricted) → **Fable 5 / Mythos 5** (2026-06-09) → **Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1** (2026-09). The structurally important event is the Mythos tier: Anthropic *withheld* a model class from general release on offensive-cyber grounds, ran it through a controlled-access programme (Project Glasswing), and then released it broadly two months later with guardrails routing high-risk bio/cyber queries to Opus 4.8. Fable 5 / Mythos 5 are priced at $10/$50 per M tokens — 2× the prior flagship. [S] [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/09/anthropic-releases-its-first-mythos-model-to-the-public/). Anthropic was reportedly subject to US export controls on its most advanced models that were later lifted — **[S] only** ([GPPi](https://gppi.net/2026/08/20/what-the-american-ai-exports-program-means-for-europe)).

### Google DeepMind
Gemini 2.5 Pro (2025-03-25) → Gemini 2.5 Deep Think (2025-08-01, IMO gold) → **Gemini 3 Pro** (2025-11-18, with the Antigravity agent IDE) → **Gemini 3.1 Pro** (~2026-02-19) → **Gemini 3.5 Flash** (2026-05-19/20 at I/O) → Gemini 3.5 Pro (~2026-06). ### Meta
**Llama 4 Scout/Maverick** (2025-04-05) was the last open-weight Meta release. After the LMArena submission controversy, Yann LeCun's departure, the ~$15B Scale AI investment (June 2025) and Alexandr Wang taking over Meta Superintelligence Labs, **Meta released Muse Spark on 2026-04-08 as a closed, API-only multimodal reasoning model** — no weight downloads, no self-hosting. Reported scores: CharXiv Reasoning **86.4** (thinking mode) vs GPT-5.4 82.8 and Gemini 3.1 Pro High 80.2; DeepSearchQA **74.8** vs Gemini 3.1 Pro High 69.7, GPT-5.4 73.6; but *below* Opus 4.6 / Gemini 3.1 Pro High / GPT-5.4 on four SWE-bench-Verified-class tests. A stronger unimplemented "contemplating mode" is claimed to beat Gemini 3.1 and GPT-5.4 Pro on no-tool HLE and FrontierScience Research. Meta's 2026 capex guidance: $115–135B. [S] [证券时报](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/3734546.html). Muse Spark 1.3 followed later in 2026, reported as roughly global #6. [S] [heise](https://www.heise.de/news/Muse-Spark-1-3-Meta-schliesst-zu-Spitzenmodellen-auf-11440233.html)

## 2. Training scaling laws in 2025–2026

**Pretraining is not dead but its *data* input is.** The canonical public statement remains Sutskever's NeurIPS 2024 talk: *"Pre-training as we know it will unquestionably end"* and *"We've achieved peak data."* [S] [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2024/12/13/24320811/what-ilya-sutskever-sees-openai-model-data-training). The crispest named-person statement of the 2024 shift is Noam Brown (OpenAI), 2024-09-12: *"The longer it thinks, the better it does on reasoning tasks"* and *"We're no longer bottlenecked by pretraining. We can now scale inference compute too."* [S] [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-o1/).

Silver & Sutton's *The Era of Experience* states: *"In key domains such as mathematics, coding, and science, the knowledge extracted from human data is rapidly approaching a limit... The pace of progress driven solely by supervised learning from human data is demonstrably slowing,"* and *"AI is at the cusp of a new period in which experience will become the dominant medium of improvement and ultimately dwarf the scale of human data used in today's systems."* Note this is a claim about **human data**, not about compute returns per se. Canonical source: [DeepMind PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf) — **it is not on arXiv** (verified: 0 arXiv results). **Date caveat:** the PDF's metadata `CreationDate` is **2025-04-10**, **no publication date is printed in the document**, the DeepMind blog URL is 404, and the sitemap has no matching entry. The document's own footnote says: *"This is a preprint of a chapter that will appear in the book Designing an Intelligence, published by MIT Press."* Cite it as an undated 2025 preprint. **[P]**

**Public disagreement about where we are.** Sam Altman at Stanford, 2026-06-21: *"Betting against LLMs scaling at this point feels quite misguided to me."* [S] [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sam-altman-says-a-whole-generation-of-researchers-held-ai-back-by-underestimating-what-scaling-could-do/). Yann LeCun, 2025-07-04: *"The era of the 'Pre-trained Scaling Myth' is coming to an end."* [S] (aggregated, not first-person) [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3364112069871367). The clearest counter-signal on test-time compute is **Epoch AI's, not OpenAI's**: their May 2025 analysis argued progress *"could slow down… as soon as within a year,"* with Josh You noting *"If there's a persistent overhead cost required for research, reasoning models might not scale as far as expected."* [S] [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/12/improvements-in-reasoning-ai-models-may-slow-down-soon-analysis-finds/). **A reported Dario Amodei episode titled "We are near the end of the exponential" (Dwarkesh Podcast, 2026-02-13) was not verifiable** — dwarkesh.com was unreachable and only third-party transcript mirrors carry it. **[!] Do not use without confirming.**

**Chinchilla still holds.** Schaeffer et al., [arXiv:2509.23963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23963) (2025-09-28): *"Can practitioners still rely on Chinchilla's prescriptions? Our work demonstrates the answer is yes."* **[P]**. But the 2026 literature is re-opening the fit: **Skaling** ([arXiv:2608.07222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07222), 2026-08-07) argues that model size and data *do not* affect loss independently, and that standard formulations *"systematically under- and overestimate loss at data-scarce and overtraining extremes"*, claiming 1.5–3× MAPE reduction with one interaction exponent. **[P]**. A 2026 paper ([arXiv:2603.22339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22339), 2026-03-21) argues Chinchilla Approach 2's IsoFLOP fit is biased even on noise-free data. **[P]**

**The data wall is quantified.** Villalobos et al. ([arXiv:2211.04325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04325), v2 2024-06-04): models will be trained on datasets roughly equal to the stock of public human text **"between 2026 and 2032."** **[P]**. Repetition is a partial substitute: Muennighoff et al. ([arXiv:2305.16264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264), v5 2025-06-28): *"up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible changes to loss... with more repetition, the value of adding compute eventually decays to zero."* **[P]**. Deliberate over-training is now standard: Llama 3 states it trained smaller models *"for much longer than is compute-optimal... The resulting models perform better than compute-optimal models at the same inference budget"*, and notes *"IsoFLOPs curves become flatter around the minimum as the compute budget increases."* **[P]** [arXiv:2407.21783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)

**RL scaling is predictable but sigmoidal, not power-law.** ScaleRL ([arXiv:2510.13786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786), 2025-10-15) used **>400,000 GPU-hours** and scaled a single run to **100,000 GPU-hours**, fitting *"sigmoidal compute-performance curves."* Its key negative result: *"loss aggregation, normalization, curriculum, and off-policy algorithm primarily modulate compute efficiency without materially shifting the asymptote."* **[P]** 2026 follow-ups: **IsoCompute Playbook** ([arXiv:2603.12151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12151), 2026-03-12) finds the compute-optimal number of parallel rollouts per problem *"increases predictably with compute budget and then saturates"*; **PrefixRL** ([arXiv:2601.18795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18795), 2026-01-26) halves steps-to-reward via off-policy prefixes. **[P] No paper found states a clean RL compute scaling exponent analogous to Chinchilla's.** **[!]**

**Test-time compute: real, but selection is the bottleneck.** s1 ([arXiv:2501.19393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393), 2025-01-31) showed budget forcing lets a 32B model beat o1-preview by up to 27% on MATH/AIME24. [arXiv:2408.00724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00724) (ICLR 2025): *"scaling inference compute... can be more computationally efficient than scaling model parameters."* **[P]** Large Language Monkeys ([arXiv:2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)) found coverage scaling over four orders of magnitude; the follow-up ([arXiv:2502.17578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17578), 2025-02-24) explained the aggregate power law as a consequence of a heavy-tailed per-problem success distribution. **[P]** The 2026 correction: [arXiv:2609.13257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13257) (2026-09-06) finds that expanding the sample pool 4→16 raises *oracle* quality by +9.23 IQ points but that *"none of twelve adaptive-depth policies outperforms uniform compute"* — sampling headroom ≠ selection gain. **[P]**

**Compute disclosure is essentially extinct — with one 2025 exception.** DeepSeek-V3 disclosed **2.788M H800 GPU-hours** for full training (671B total / 37B active, 14.8T tokens) — [arXiv:2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437). Llama 3 405B disclosed **3.8×10²⁵ FLOPs** — [arXiv:2407.21783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783). **OpenAI's gpt-oss paper discloses 2.1M H100-hours** for gpt-oss-120b/20b — [arXiv:2508.10925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10925), the only OpenAI compute figure in this window. **Verified absences:** Kimi K2 ([arXiv:2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)), Qwen3 ([arXiv:2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)), the **GPT-5 system card** ([PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/gpt-5-system-card.pdf), dated 2025-08-13 — which contains **no architecture, parameter count or context-window disclosure at all**), and **every 2026 frontier model from every lab**. [P] verified absences.

### Coding and agents
**SWE-bench Verified is dead as a frontier metric.** Trajectory: Claude 3.7 Sonnet 63.7% (2025-02-24) → Opus 4 72.5% (2025-05-22) → Sonnet 4.5 77.2% (2025-09-29) → Opus 4.5 ~80.9% **[S]** (2025-11-24) → **93.9% "saturated"** as declared by SWE-bench co-creator Ofir Press, attributed to Claude Mythos Preview (2026-04) **[S] only**. OpenAI formally retracted it (~2026-02): it audited a **27.6% subset** (138 of 500 problems o3 consistently failed) and found **≥59.4% of the audited problems have flawed test cases that reject functionally correct submissions**; every frontier model tested reproduced the **gold patch verbatim**, indicating training contamination. OpenAI now recommends SWE-bench Pro. [S] [HN thread](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47910388) — the openai.com page 403s to fetchers, so this is **secondary-corroborated only**.

The critique literature is primary and strong: *The SWE-Bench Illusion* ([arXiv:2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286)) — models identify buggy file paths from the issue text alone with up to **76%** accuracy vs up to 53% on non-SWE-bench repos, and verbatim 5-gram similarity reaches **35%** on Verified/Full vs 18% elsewhere; *SWE-Bench+* ([arXiv:2410.06992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992)) — **32.67%** solution leakage and **31.08%** weak-test patches; filtering dropped SWE-Agent+GPT-4 from **12.47% → 3.97%**. **[P]**

**SWE-bench Pro** (Scale AI, [arXiv:2509.16941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941), v1 2025-09-21): 1,865 problems / 41 repos; public 731, commercial 276, held-out 858. Public set at 2025-09-18: **Sonnet 4.5 43.6%, Sonnet 4 42.7%, GPT-5 (high) 41.8%, Haiku 4.5 39.5%**, Kimi K2 27.7%, gpt-oss-120b 16.2%. Commercial set: Opus 4.1 17.8%, GPT-5 high 15.7%, GPT-4o 3.6%. The paper frames itself as measuring *"beyond the saturation of SWE-bench Verified."* 2026 vendor numbers: GPT-5.5 **58.6%** [S]; MiniMax-M2.7 **56.2%** [P]; Qwen3.8-Max **67.7%**, Claude Opus 4.8 69.2%, Claude Fable 5 **80.0%** [S]. Scale's leaderboard is Cloudflare-blocked, so the 2026 frontier number is **[!] unverified**. Pro is itself audited: *A Determinacy Audit of SWE-bench Pro* (June Kim, 2026-06-09, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20738219) finds a proven floor of **109/728 (15.0%)** of the public set underdetermined, plus 3 golds that fail their own grader. **[P]**

Notable design choices: DeepSeek-V3 uses **aux-loss-free load balancing** (sigmoid affinity + per-expert bias) and multi-token prediction; Qwen3 uses global-batch load balancing and **no shared experts**; Kimi K2 trains with **MuonClip** (Muon + QK-clip) and reports **zero loss spikes over 15.5T tokens**. [P]

**Attention: MLA → DSA → intra-Transformer hybridisation.** DeepSeek's Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) compressed the KV cache in V2/V3 and was adopted by Kimi K2/K3 (as *Gated MLA*). **DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)** shipped in V3.2-Exp (2025-09-29) — a lightning indexer selects top-k tokens, reducing core attention from O(L²) to O(Lk) at benchmark parity with V3.1 — and has since been **adopted by Zhipu in GLM-5**. [P] [DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp repo](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp). 2026 refinements: **IndexCache** ([arXiv:2603.12201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12201), 2026-03-12) removes 75% of indexer compute for 1.82× prefill / 1.48× decode; **MiniMax Sparse Attention** ([arXiv:2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392), 2026-06-11) is blockwise sparse over GQA with **28.4× less per-token attention compute at 1M context**. [P]

Linear/hybrid attention is now mainstream: **Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B** (2025-09-11) and **Qwen3.5** combine **Gated Delta Networks with sparse MoE**; **Kimi Linear** ([arXiv:2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692), 2025-10-30) defines **KDA** (Gated DeltaNet + finer channel-wise gating), beating full MLA at 3B-activated/48B-total with 75% less KV cache and 6× faster decode at 1M; **Kimi K3** interleaves 69 KDA with 24 Gated MLA layers (3:1) plus "Attention Residuals". [P]. **gpt-oss** alternates banded (bandwidth-128) and fully dense attention layers with 8 KV heads. [P]. Hybrid SSM/Transformer designs remain mid-scale: Jamba ([arXiv:2403.19887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887)), Nemotron-H ([arXiv:2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624)), Falcon-H1 ([arXiv:2507.22448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448)), Hunyuan-TurboS ([arXiv:2505.15431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15431)). [P]

One counter-trend worth a slide: **MiniMax's M2 series reverted to full GQA on every layer** (`attn_type_list` all 1s) despite M1 having pioneered lightning attention — i.e. a linear-attention lab went back to full attention for agentic workloads. [P] [SemiAnalysis InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/model/minimax-m27)

**Long context.** Advertised windows: Gemini 2.0 Pro 2M (Feb 2025), Gemini 2.5 / 3 Pro 1M input (3 Pro is 1M in / 64K out) [P] Google model cards; Llama 4 Scout 10M; MiniMax 1M native; Kimi K3 1,048,576. By Sept 2026, **1M is the default flagship window** at OpenAI and Anthropic (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, Opus 5). **No verified 2026 frontier model exceeds 1M context.** [P] Contrary evidence on effective context: RULER ([arXiv:2404.06654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654)) and NoLiMa ([arXiv:2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)) both show advertised windows far exceed usable context. [P]

**Low precision.** DeepSeek-V3 was the first frontier-scale **FP8** training run (block-wise quantization, FP8 activation dispatch, BF16 optimizer states; validated on ~1T tokens with <0.25% relative loss error; ~34% throughput gain) — [P] [arXiv:2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437). **Correction worth making on a slide:** *"Scaling FP8 training to trillion-token LLMs"* is [arXiv:2409.12517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12517), originally **2024-09-19** — the widely cited "Feb 2025" date is its v2 revision. FP8 is now the default in open weights (MiniMax-M2.x ship `float8_e4m3fn`, 128×128 blocks). **MXFP4** is used by gpt-oss — but it is **post-training quantization of the MoE weights only**, with all other tensors in BF16. [P] The strongest verified **FP4 training** claim is **Kimi K3: MXFP4 weights + MXFP8 activations with quantization-aware training from SFT onward**. [P]. Foundational NVFP4 pretraining work: [arXiv:2509.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149) (2025-09-29). [P]

**muP / hyperparameter transfer** — **no named 2026 frontier model has a verifiable muP claim.** 2026 work is academic and MoE-specific ([arXiv:2605.14200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14200), GQA-μP [arXiv:2605.15290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15290)). Foundations: Tensor Programs V ([arXiv:2203.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03466)), Cerebras-GPT ([arXiv:2304.03208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03208)). The nearest large-scale 2026 empirical result is a hyperparameter scaling law across MoE sparsity from 1,800 pretraining runs (~20T tokens, *"200,000 equivalent H800 GPU-hours"*), [arXiv:2609.08690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08690). [P]

**Interpretability.** Dated primary milestones: **Scaling Monosemanticity** 2024-05-21; **OpenAI SAEs for GPT-4** [arXiv:2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093) 2024-06-06; **auditing hidden objectives** 2025-03-13; **circuit tracing / attribution graphs** (cross-layer transcoders, Claude 3.5 Haiku) **2025-03-27**; **Petri** (open-source audit-agent toolkit, used in Claude 4 / Sonnet 4.5 system cards and by UK AISI) **2025-10-06**; **introspection** 2025-10-29; **"A global workspace in language models"** (J-space, via Jacobians) 2026-07-06. [P] anthropic.com/research/*, [transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html). The May 2026 *Circuits Updates* post is explicitly **preliminary lab-meeting-grade notes**, not a paper; its single post argues that top-activating examples plus logit-lens unembeds are insufficient descriptors, because they group features that look similar but have different causal effects — **downstream connections predict which CLT features actually steer behaviour**. [P] [Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/may-update/)

**Post-Transformer:** **no verified 2026 frontier model is not a Transformer.** Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-2.4T, GLM-5.x, MiniMax-M3 and Gemini 3 Pro are all **sparse MoE Transformers**; the real change is hybridisation *inside* the Transformer (KDA/Gated-MLA interleaving, sparse attention, attention residuals), not replacement. [P]. The interesting non-Transformer work is all small: **Mamba-3** ([arXiv:2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569), ICLR 2026) is evaluated at 1.5B; **HRM-Text** ([arXiv:2605.20613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20613), 2026-05-20) is 1B params trained on 40B tokens for ~$1,500. One genuinely novel axis: **DeepSeek Engram** (repo 2026-01-12) adds N-gram lookup memory as a *complementary* sparsity axis to MoE, with a U-shaped scaling law, on a Transformer backbone. [P] Diffusion LMs (Mercury Coder Feb 2025; **Mercury 2**, second-fastest on Artificial Analysis) are **[S] only** — no primary confirmation obtained. [P]/[S]

## 5. Open-weight vs closed gap

**Who leads open weights, Sept 2026:** Moonshot (Kimi K2.6 → K3, 2.8T), Zhipu/Z.ai (GLM-5.3), Alibaba (Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B), DeepSeek (V4-Pro 1.6T/49B, V4.1-Flash 552B), MiniMax (M2.7), ByteDance (Seed), NVIDIA (Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B), OpenAI (gpt-oss-120b), Google (Gemma 4). **Meta has left the category entirely** — Muse Spark is closed. [S] [DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/models/text-generation) / [P] Epoch AI model database

**The measured gap has narrowed, then widened slightly.** Epoch AI's time series is the cleanest measurement:
- **Nov 2024**: best open model on par with closed models at a lag of ~**one year**. [P] [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/open-models-report)
- **Oct 2025**: open weights lag SOTA by ~**3 months** on average; average ECI gap ~**7 points**, comparable to the gap between o3 and GPT-5. [P] [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-weights-vs-closed-weights-models)
- **May 2026**: since January 2026 the best open-weight models have lagged frontier closed models by ~**4 months** on average; average ECI gap ~**8 points**, comparable to the GPT-5 → GPT-5.5 gap. Best open (Kimi K2.6, ECI 151.60) vs best closed (GPT-5.5 Pro xhigh, 159.35) is **~7.8 ECI points**. [P] [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap)

**Benchmark deltas, late 2026** (vendor-reported, [S]): Qwen3.8-Max scores **PaperBench 93.0** and **OSWorld-Verified 86.1** but trails on **TerminalBench 2.1 (86.6 vs GPT-5.6 Sol 88.8)**, **SWE-bench Pro (67.7 vs Claude Fable 5 80.0, Opus 4.8 69.2)** and **VideoMME v2 (68.3 vs 71.1)**. A Sept 2026 arXiv paper ([arXiv:2609.00949](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00949)) finds that on public tool-calling benchmarks *"open-weight models now approach or even surpass closed-source frontier models in aggregate accuracy,"* with calibration as the residual gap. **[P]**

**Cost is the sharpest divergence.** DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 lists at **$0.06/$0.18 per M tokens**, GLM-5.3-Flash at **$0.075/$0.25**, versus GPT-5.5 at $5/$30 and Fable 5 at $10/$50. In OpenDesign Arena (2026-09-10), DeepSeek V4.1 Flash scored **81.2 vs GPT-6 Astra's 82.7 (~98%)** at **$0.023 vs $1.61 per finished design (~1.4% of cost)**. [S] [DigitalToday](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/102509/deepseek-v4-1-flash-nears-gpt-6-astra-performance-at-1-4-percent-of-cost) / [P] [DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/models/text-generation). Epoch earlier estimated DeepSeek-R1 rivalled o1 at **~30× lower cost**. [P] [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/topics/open-models)

**Policy.** The US AI Action Plan (2025-07-23) contains a dedicated *"Encourage Open-Source and Open-Weight AI"* pillar, arguing open models have *"unique value for innovation"* and that release decisions are *"fundamentally up to the developer"*; the same document directs NIST/CAISI to evaluate Chinese frontier models for alignment with CCP talking points. [P] [whitehouse.gov PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf). By 2026 the **American AI Exports Program** bars consortia from using open-weight models from *"countries of concern"* — i.e. DeepSeek, Qwen and Kimi — and requires ≥51% US-made hardware value. [S] [GPPi](https://gppi.net/2026/08/20/what-the-american-ai-exports-program-means-for-europe)

**Open weights as an input to other models:** Salesforce **Koa** was built by RL post-training the open-weight Nemotron-3-Super-120B ([arXiv:2609.15066](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15066), 2026-09-14). **[P]**

## 6. Reliability notes — what NOT to put on a slide

1. **LMArena ranks — [!/unverified].** `lmarena.ai` was unreachable. A circulating "closed models outperform open-weight by 29 Elo points in 2026" figure appears only on an exchange news-flash page ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/closed-ai-models-outperform-open-weight-models-by-29-elo-points-in-2026)) — **SEO-grade, do not cite.**
2. **Terminal-Bench 2.0 scores** come from secondary aggregators that mis-cite paper IDs. TB2.0's existence, Harbor harness and ICLR 2026 paper *are* verified from the official repo.
3. **SWE-bench Verified 93.9% for Claude Mythos Preview (Apr 2026)** is secondary-only; Anthropic's own table is an image.
4. **SWE-bench Pro 2026 SOTA (~45–80%)** — the Scale leaderboard is Cloudflare-blocked; all 2026 Pro numbers above are vendor self-reports.
5. **All Codeforces Elo figures** — no primary source found (OpenAI's o3/o4-mini and GPT-5 system cards contain zero occurrences of "Codeforces"). **Do not report any.**
6. **IOI 2026 "first AI to beat the top human"** rests on one unrefereed self-reported preprint ([arXiv:2609.02849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02849)); **ICPC WF 2026: nothing found.**
7. **The o1 quote is misquoted almost everywhere.** The real sentence is *"the performance of o1 consistently improves with more reinforcement learning (train-time compute) and with more time spent thinking (test-time compute)."* The short form circulating as "performance consistently improves" drops both the subject and the adverb. Verified verbatim only from [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/machines/openai-o1-reasoning-ai-model-preview) (2024-09-13), because `openai.com` serves HTTP 403 (Cloudflare) to non-browser clients. The post URL and date *are* confirmed from OpenAI's own feed: `openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms`, 2024-09-12.
8. **There is no December 2024 o3 blog post on openai.com.** Verified against OpenAI's own [news RSS](https://openai.com/news/rss.xml): the earliest o3 items are o3-mini (2025-01-31) and o3/o4-mini (2025-04-16); the only 2024-12-20 item is "Deliberative alignment." o3 was announced on the live "12 Days of OpenAI" stream. **Cite the [ARC Prize post](https://arcprize.org/blog/oai-o3-pub-breakthrough) instead** — it is a better primary source anyway (75.7% semi-private at the $10k limit; 87.5% at 172× compute).
9. **"The Era of Experience" has no publication date.** PDF metadata `CreationDate` is 2025-04-10; nothing is printed in the document; the DeepMind blog URL is 404 and the sitemap has no matching entry. The document describes itself only as *"a preprint of a chapter that will appear in the book Designing an Intelligence, published by MIT Press."* Cite as an undated 2025 preprint.
10. **No RL compute scaling exponent exists in the literature.** ScaleRL fits sigmoids only.
11. **The Amodei "10% chance scaling stagnates" quote is a trap.** It traces to a **July 2023** podcast, not 2025–26; the episode exists but no accessible transcript contains the sentence; and the preprint citing it ([arXiv:2508.05619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05619)) uses a generic NYT section URL rather than an episode permalink — that same preprint's acknowledgements state its citations were validated by LLM search tools. **Do not present it as a current statement.** A possible 2026 replacement, Dwarkesh Podcast 2026-02-13, *"We are near the end of the exponential,"* surfaced only via third-party transcript mirrors (dwarkesh.com unreachable) — **confirm before use.** The 2025–2026 lab-leader quotes that *are* verified are Altman (2026-06-21, *"Betting against LLMs scaling at this point feels quite misguided to me"*), LeCun (2025-07-04, *"The era of the 'Pre-trained Scaling Myth' is coming to an end"*), and Noam Brown (2024-09-12). A Demis Hassabis 20VC episode is *titled* "…We Have Not Hit Scaling Laws…" but **no verbatim quote is retrievable** — title only, do not quote.
11. **DeepSeek V4 — a genuine conflict between sources.** Multiple news outlets (Chinese and Spanish) report a **V4 *preview* going live on 2026-04-24** as a service with 1M context and two models ([iheima](https://www.iheima.com/article-396620.html), [Europapress](https://www.europapress.es/portaltic/sector/noticia-deepseek-v4-llega-dos-nuevos-modelos-ofrecen-alto-rendimiento-contextos-largos-coste-reducido-20260424103314.html)), and a hosting catalogue lists `DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813` (1.6T/49B). **However, a direct check of the `deepseek-ai` GitHub organisation found no V4 repository or technical report as of 2026-09-10.** So: "DeepSeek V4 exists as an API product" is well-supported; "DeepSeek V4 is open-weight, MIT-licensed, 1.6T/49B" is **unconfirmed**. Do not present the parameter counts as fact.
12. **Kimi K2.6 — conflicting.** A credible NYU Shanghai summary describes it (2026-04-20, 256K context, 300-agent swarms), but no Kimi K2.6 artifact was found in Moonshot's repositories. Kimi **K3** (2.8T/104B, 896 experts, 1,048,576 context, MXFP4/MXFP8 QAT) **is** primary-verified from the tech report. [P] [Kimi-K3 repo](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-K3)
13. **DeepSeek V4's MIT licence** and the other licence strings this briefing could not read at model-card level are **unconfirmed**.
14. **Model parameter counts and training compute for every closed 2026 frontier model are undisclosed.** Do not present estimates as facts.
15. **AI-generated SEO aggregators were deliberately excluded** (benchlm.ai, aiwiki.ai, llm-stats.com, orcarouter.ai, fastino.ai, swfte.com). One of them publishes a "top verified HLE" figure that **contradicts the official HLE board** — if a search result cites one of these, discard it.
