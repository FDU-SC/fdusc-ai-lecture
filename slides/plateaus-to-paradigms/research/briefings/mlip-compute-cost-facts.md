## (a) Training cost
- **MACE-MP-0 / MPA-0** ([2401.00096v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00096v3)): "The original MACE-MP-0a models were trained for 200 epochs with 40–80 NVIDIA A100 GPUs across 10–20 nodes on … Perlmutter"; "The updated MACE-MP-0b3 models is trained for 99 epochs with 32 NVIDIA H100 GPUs across 8 nodes on the Jean Zay cluster"; Methods: "Model is trained for 100 epochs on 40–80 NVIDIA H100 GPUs across 10–20 nodes. Training the medium-sized model took approx. 2,600 GPU hours." MPA-0 has **no separate figure** — "identical hyperparameters to the MP-0b3 medium model but trained on an expanded dataset combining MPtraj and sAlex" (sAlex = 10.5 M structures).
- **UMA** ([2506.23971](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23971v1), Table 8, H200 140GB/H100 80GB × days): UMA-S 128×5 + 256×5 = 46,080 H200 GPU-h; UMA-M 128×14 + 256×14 = **129,024 H200 GPU-h** (independently recomputed in DPA4); UMA-L 128×25 + 128×4 + 128×2 = 95,232 H100 GPU-h. Trained on ~500 M structures.
- **eSEN** ([2502.12147](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12147v2)): authors report no GPU-hours; DPA4 ([2606.02419v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02419v3)) states "eSEN-30M-MP … using 335 A100 GPU-days", "no public training cost is reported for the eSEN baselines".
- **CHGNet** ([2302.14231v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14231v2)): 400,438 params, batch 40, 20 epochs — no GPU type/hours. **M3GNet** ([2202.02450v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.02450v2)): no training compute. **Orb-v2** ([2410.22570](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22570v1)): "8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs", finetune on 1 A100. **Orb-v3** ([2504.06231](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06231v2)): none. **MatterSim** ([2405.04967v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04967v2)): M3GNet-based 200 epochs, batch 128, 8 A100; Graphormer on 64 A100. **SevenNet-0** ([2402.03789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03789)): "four A100 GPUs with a batch size of 16". **MatterGen** ([2312.03687v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03687v2)): "1.74 million steps with a batch size of 64 per GPU over eight A100 GPUs".
- **GNoME**: I could **not verify** any "~5 million GPU-hours" or "100×" figure — the Nature paper reports only 4 TPU v3 chips and "hundreds of millions of first-principles calculations".

## (b) Inference
- UMA ([Table 1/3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23971v1), single H100, 50 neighbours/atom, 6 Å, graph-gen excluded) steps/s at 100 / 1k / 10k / 50k / 100k atoms: UMA-S 44/16/1.6/0.2/0.1; UMA-M 21/3/0.2/OOM; eSEN-30M-OAM 8/1.7/OOM; Orb-v3-conservative-inf-omat 77/30/3.7/OOM; MACE-MPA-0 38/24/2.9/OOM; MACE-OFF23-L 89/20/OOM. "UMA-S can simulate 1000 atoms at 16 steps per second (1.4 ns-per-day)".
- MACE-MP-0 ([2401.00096v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00096v3)): "on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU with 80GB of RAM, it can do several nanoseconds per day for 1000 atoms"; weak scaling at 0.1 ns/day "perfect up to 32,000 atoms and 64 GPUs".
- Orb-v3 ([2504.06231v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06231v2)): H200, 1k atoms, steps/s — MACE-MPA-0 21.2, MatterSim v1 5M 18.8, SevenNet-MF-ompa 3.5; "orb-v3-direct-20 uses only 32.8GB of GPU memory and completes in under half a second" at 100,000 atoms; direct models "≥10× reduction in latency and ≥8× reduction in memory".
- eSEN ([2502.12147v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12147v2)): 216 atoms on one A100 — 0.4 / 0.8 M steps/day (eSEN 3.2M/6.4M) vs MACE-OFF-L 0.7 M.
- GNoME ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06735-9)): "Inference on an A100 GPU on a 50-atom system takes approximately 14 ms, enabling a throughput of approximately 12 ns day⁻¹ at a 2-fs time step".
- SevenNet ([2402.03789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03789)): 60 ps/100,000-atom melt-quench in 12.7 h on 32 A100s (~0.1 ns/day); 14,000 atoms on 1 A100: 60 ps in 12.0 h. OMat24 eqV2 throughput on A100 = 9.4/7.4/4.9 samples per GPU-s (S/M/L) ([2410.12771v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12771v1)).

## (c) "x million × faster than DFT" — what is actually compared
I found **no primary-source "10⁶×" claim** in these papers. Verified claims: M3GNet, "This relaxation can be performed on a laptop in about 22 seconds on a single CPU core … while the corresponding DFT relaxation took 15 hours on 32 cores" (single relaxation, same cell ⇒ ~7.9×10⁴ in core-seconds); and "at least four orders of magnitude faster than the DFT calculations" for SiO₂ phonons — an acknowledged **apples-to-oranges** comparison ("The model calculations used a frozen phonon approach while the original calculations used DFPT… The supercell size difference also explains the differences") ([2202.02450v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.02450v2)). The 23-model benchmark makes the methodological point explicitly: "DFT cost grows approximately cubically with atom count, whereas most fixed-cutoff MLFFs … scale close to linearly. Thus, DFT-normalized speedups provide a practical reference scale, but the more rigorous comparison is between MLFFs and classical force fields under the same atomistic workload" — their 192-atom LiCoO₂/PBE baseline gives Buckingham ~11,600×, 2NNMEAM+QEq ~533×, lightweight MLIPs "hundreds to thousands", heaviest MLIPs "<2×" ([2607.07647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07647)). UMA states the scaling basis: "O(n) vs. O(n³) for DFT … reducing computation time from hours to less than a second" ([2506.23971](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23971v1)).

## (d) Benchmarks, scaling, long-range
- MLIP Arena ([2509.20630v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20630v2)): runtime = "MD steps per second on a single A100 GPU", 1 CPU core + 1 A100 per run; "ORBv2 is the fastest and has the best scaling exponent"; MatterSim ≠ M3GNet speed despite same architecture. MatSciML ([2309.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05934)); LAMBench ([2504.19578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19578)).
- Matbench Discovery's **CPS is "Combined Performance Score", not a throughput metric** ([2308.14920v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.14920v3)); DPA4 plots CPS *against* A100 GPU-days ([2606.02419v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02419v3)).
- Memory scales ~linearly in atoms at fixed cutoff: Orb-v3 32.8 GB @100k atoms; UMA-S 100k+ atoms in 80 GB. MACE-MP-0 explicitly lacks long-range physics: "does not contain explicit long-range interactions (beyond the 12 Å receptive field …), nor does it take into account magnetic or spin degrees of freedom"; UMA likewise: with a 6 Å cutoff "if an adsorbate starts at 7 Å from a catalyst's surface the model views these as two independent non-interacting structures".
